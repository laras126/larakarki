Hi, I’m {{ site.author.name }}

Ph.D. candidate in Human-Centered Computing at Georgia Tech, studying the future of computing work

I’m an NSF Graduate Research Fellow in Atlanta, GA, where I work with Dr. Betsy DiSalvo and the DataWorks team. Before my PhD, I worked as a self-taught software developer for a decade, and encounters with gatekeeping culture motivated my career change to research. Some of my earlier work is published under Lara Schenck.

My research focuses on how broadening our concept of computing work to include forms of everyday computation across work contexts can open new pathways to expertise and employment, and new perspectives on work and labor in the “age of AI.” I use participatory and community-engaged methods to study authentic workplaces in collaboration with workers.

I am on the job market this year, seeking tenure-track positions starting fall 2027.

{{ site.data.research_questions.overarching }}

When you picture a computing worker, what do you see? Many people will picture a software developing writing code in text-based, general purpose programming languages.

My inquiry into broadening computing work expands to four research areas that emerged from my engagements DataWorks during a three-year embedded ethnography during my PhD research, where I founded and facilitate a career development program for five cohorts and 15 workers.
